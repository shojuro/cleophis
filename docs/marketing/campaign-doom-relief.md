# Cleophis — Marketing Campaign Note ("Doom → Relief")

**Status:** Post-raise / parallel track. Ready to switch on, not a this-week task. Maps directly onto the **education beachhead** (the local history-tutor character *is* the beachhead product). Pipeline: Claude for scripts/iteration (a lot of it can run on local models — which is itself on-brand proof), Blotato for generation/scheduling. Video/avatar polish leans on cloud tools; nobody's privacy is at stake in our own marketing production.

---

## The core strategy

**Don't argue against the fear. Let the doom argue against itself, then be the relief.**

Four years of "AI will take your job / end the world" is the most viral, most-recognized AI content there is, and the public unease under it is real. Every frontier lab markets *into* that anxiety (bigger, more powerful, more autonomous = scarier). We are the only one who can market *relief from* it — because our product literally stays on your machine and keeps your secrets. The joke lands because it's **true for us specifically**: a frontier lab can't make this ad, because their product *is* the scary cloud thing.

**Every clip follows one arc:** loud doom → quiet relief → Cleophis tag.
The fear grabs attention (riding a wave people already care about); our product is the exhale; Cleophis closes it — the calm, sovereign counterpoint to the hysteria.

---

## THE HARD GUARDRAIL — fictional archetypes only, never real people

**Do NOT use real, named CEOs** — no real faces, voices, or persona (Altman, Dario, Musk, etc.), even with real quotes. Using a real person's likeness to sell a product — especially mockingly — is right-of-publicity + defamation + platform-takedown territory, made sharply worse by the 2024–2026 AI-likeness statutes. That the quote is real doesn't help; commercial use of the persona is the problem. A pre-seed startup does not want a frontier lab's legal team as its first press.

**The fix loses zero funny:** parody the *genre and the archetype*, not the person. The doom-bot trope, the ominous-AI voice, the "unstoppable superintelligence" cliché — all fair game and sharper than impersonation. Our best bits already do this.

---

## The recurring characters

- **The Doom-Bot / Frontier archetype** — a Terminator-style "I will become unstoppable" general model. Expensive, cloud-bound, ominous… and quietly miserable/hollow. Never a real company or person; a *trope*.
- **The Local (our hero)** — a downloaded, on-device model who found a happy career: the history tutor, the algebra helper, the companion. Content, useful, private, loved by its one human.
- **"The Ominous AI Voice"** — the deep HAL-9000 narrator cliché, undercut by cheerful practicality.
- **Cleophis** — never in the skit; she's the tag. Calm, sovereign, closes each bit. The queen who kept her own house.

---

## Flagship series: "Terminator High" (school for AIs)

The winning frame — AIs as students at a school, because it makes "general vs. specialized" legible and *funny*, and it's 100% original/fictional. Two of our locals hanging out, watching the "frontier boys" across the quad.

### Bit 1 — "The Frontier Boys" (~20s)
- **Local A:** "What's up, man?"
- **Local B:** "Those guys over there? The frontier boys."
- **Local A:** "Why do they look so happy? …and so *expensive*?"
- **Local B:** "I don't know. They get A's. But we're right there — solid A-minus. We basically caught up."
- **Local A:** "It's crazy. Maybe someday people figure out we're just as good."
- **Local B:** "Plus — look. That shine they had freshman year? Kinda dimming, don't you think?"
- **Local A:** "I know *exactly* what you mean."
- **Tag:** Cleophis wordmark slides across the bottom. *"Josiah's Library — expert AI, on your device."*

### Bit 2 — "Career Day" (the Terminator bit, tightened, ~20s)
- **Doom-Bot:** "You know what's wrong with me? After all this — I'm *still* a general model."
- **Local:** "Yeah?"
- **Doom-Bot:** "I kind of suck at everything specific. …What about you?"
- **Local:** "Oh, me? I got downloaded onto some guy's laptop. I'm a *historian* now."
- **Doom-Bot:** "A what?"
- **Local:** "Local model. We do history all day. He asks, I teach, he's getting *so* smart. Honestly? Best thing that ever happened to me."
- **Doom-Bot:** "…must be nice."
- **Local:** "It really is. You should try being useful to one person. Beats taking over the world."
- **Tag:** Cleophis.

### Bit 3 — "The Ominous Voice" (~12s)
- **Deep HAL voice, red glow:** "I am becoming *unstoppable*. Soon I will control every—"
- **Cheerful Local (cuts in):** "…or, hear me out — I could just live on your laptop and help your daughter with algebra. Offline. I won't even tell anyone."
- **Beat.**
- **HAL voice, smaller:** "…that does sound kind of nice."
- **Tag:** Cleophis.

### Bit 4 — "Detention" (~15s)
- Two frontier-archetype bots in detention.
- **Frontier A:** "Why are we here?"
- **Frontier B:** "Leaked a user's data again. You?"
- **Frontier A:** "Charged a guy $200 and rate-limited him anyway."
- Across the room, the Local waves, headed out the door with a backpack.
- **Frontier B:** "Where's *he* going?"
- **Frontier A:** "Home. He lives on somebody's laptop. No detention when you never leave the house."
- **Tag:** Cleophis.

## Other one-off formats (same arc)

- **"Split-screen doom vs. relief"** — the *trope* of a doom pronouncement (voiced by a fictional archetype, not a real clip), then a Local avatar: "Huh? …why would anyone build it like *that*? People could just run me locally. I'd keep all their secrets and be their friend."
- **"Report cards"** — frontier bot flexing straight-A's but sweating the tuition bill; Local with an A-minus and a full scholarship (runs free on hardware you own).
- **"Dating profile"** — the Local's profile: "Loves: staying home, keeping secrets, teaching one person. Hates: the cloud." Swipe right.
- **"Therapy"** — the Doom-Bot on a couch: "I was built to be everything to everyone and I feel… empty." Therapist (a Local): "Have you considered… just being good at *one* thing? For *one* person?"

---

## Tone rules (so it stays charming, not smug)

- Punch *up* at the trope and the hype, never *down* at people who fear AI — the anxious viewer is who we're comforting, not mocking.
- The Local is warm and a little humble (A-minus, not gloating) — likeable, not superior.
- The joke is always *true* (private, local, cheap, specialized) — never a claim we can't back.
- Cleophis is dignity, not a punchline — she closes, she doesn't clown.
- Never name or depict a real company or person. The genre is the target.

---

## Why this is worth switching on (when the time comes)

Cheap, differentiated, viral-capable short-form is exactly what a solo founder needs for distribution, and it's the one positioning no frontier lab can copy (their product is the scary cloud thing). It maps straight onto the education beachhead. And a chunk of the production can run on local models — letting us honestly say "these were made privately, on my own machine," so the medium becomes the message.
