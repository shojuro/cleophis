# Pepto-Bismol (bismuth subsalicylate)

Pepto-Bismol (bismuth subsalicylate) is a medicine that treats symptoms of indigestion, heartburn and diarrhoea. You can buy it from pharmacies and shops.

## What Pepto-Bismol is for

Pepto-Bismol (bismuth subsalicylate) is used to treat symptoms such as:

- indigestion
- acid reflux and heartburn
- feeling sick (nausea)
- diarrhoea

It works by reducing inflammation and forming a protective coating in your stomach, the lower part of your food pipe and your gut.

## How to take Pepto-Bismol

Pepto-Bismol (bismuth subsalicylate) usually comes as a liquid you swallow or tablets you chew. You can take it with or without food.

Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.

### How to take different types of Pepto-Bismol

**Liquid (17.5 mg/ml oral suspension)**

When taking liquid Pepto-Bismol:

- shake the bottle before use
- take 30ml at a time using the dosing cup that comes with your medicine
- wait at least 30 minutes between doses
- do not take more than 8 doses in 24 hours

**Tablets (262.5mg chewable tablets)**

When taking Pepto-Bismol tablets:

- chew 2 tablets, or dissolve them in your mouth
- wait at least 30 minutes between doses
- do not take more than 16 tablets in 24 hours

### Important

Do not take Pepto-Bismol for more than 2 days unless advised to by a doctor.

Stop taking it and see a GP if your symptoms get worse or do not get better.

### If you take too much Pepto-Bismol

Taking an extra dose of Pepto-Bismol is unlikely to cause problems, but it's important to get medical advice if you take too much as it can be dangerous.

#### Urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist as soon as possible if:

- you've taken more Pepto-Bismol than it says on the packet or leaflet
- you're taking Pepto-Bismol and start hearing ringing or buzzing in your ears (tinnitus)

If you're unable to speak to a pharmacist, or you took too much of your medicine on purpose, call NHS 111 for advice.

If you're told to go to A&E, do not drive. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance.

Bring the Pepto-Bismol packet or leaflet inside it, any remaining medicine, and any other medicines you take.

## Side effects of Pepto-Bismol

Like all medicines, Pepto-Bismol (bismuth subsalicylate) can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.

### Common side effects

Possible side effects of Pepto-Bismol include:

- black poo
- a black tongue

These side effects are harmless and usually go away on their own.

Pepto-Bismol will not usually affect your ability to drive, ride a bike or use machinery.

#### Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:

- you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away

They may be able to give you advice about how to reduce any side effects.

### Serious side effects

In rare cases, Pepto-Bismol can cause a serious allergic reaction.

Symptoms of an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) include a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash and difficulty breathing.

#### Immediate action required: Call 999 if:

- you're taking Pepto-Bismol and think you might be having anaphylaxis

### More about side effects

You can report any side effects you get on the Yellow Card Scheme website

## Who can and cannot take Pepto-Bismol

Most people aged 16 years or older can take Pepto-Bismol (bismuth subsalicylate).

Do not give Pepto-Bismol to children aged 15 years or under because it can cause a serious condition called Reye's syndrome in rare cases.

Pepto-Bismol is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if you:

- have had an allergic reaction or serious side effect from bismuth subsalicylate before
- have had an allergic reaction or serious side effect from aspirin before
- have blood in your poo
- have a blood clotting disorder
- have gout
- have a stomach ulcer

Check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine to see if it's suitable for you. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if you're not sure if you can use it.

### Breastfeeding and Pepto-Bismol

Pepto-Bismol is not usually recommended if you're breastfeeding.

Check with a pharmacist or doctor before using Pepto-Bismol while you're breastfeeding. They may be able to recommend a different medicine.

### Pregnancy and Pepto-Bismol

Pepto-Bismol is not usually recommended during pregnancy.

Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before taking Pepto-Bismol while you're pregnant. They may be able to recommend a different medicine.

## Taking Pepto-Bismol with other medicines, food and drink

There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with Pepto-Bismol (bismuth subsalicylate).

These include:

- non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), such as aspirin and ibuprofen
- anticoagulants, such as warfarin and apixaban
- methotrexate, a medicine for rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis or cancer
- some antibiotics, such as doxycycline
- some antidepressants
- some medicines for gout
- some medicines for diabetes

Check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine to see if it's suitable for you. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if you're not sure if you can take it.

### Food, drink and alcohol with Pepto-Bismol

You can eat and drink as usual when taking Pepto-Bismol.
