# Adalimumab

Common brands: Amgevita, Humira, Imraldi, Yuflyma

Adalimumab is a medicine used to treat conditions caused by a problem with your immune system, such as rheumatoid arthritis and Crohn's disease. It's usually given by a specialist after other medicines have not worked.

## What adalimumab is for

Adalimumab is used to treat conditions that can happen when the immune system attacks healthy parts of the body by mistake (autoimmune conditions).

Adalimumab is used to treat:

- skin conditions such as psoriasis and hidradenitis suppurativa
- rheumatoid arthritis
- digestive conditions such as Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis
- uveitis (a type of autoimmune eye condition)

Adalimumab is a type of medicine called a biologic disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drug (DMARD). DMARDs work by blocking the parts of the immune system that cause swelling (inflammation) in your body, which helps relieve your symptoms.

Adalimumab contains living cells that help prevent your immune system from damaging your own healthy tissue.

## How to take adalimumab

Adalimumab comes in a pre-filled syringe or injector pen (auto-injector) that you inject into the skin of your belly or your inner thigh.

Depending on why you're taking adalimumab, you may need to have the injection once every 1 or 2 weeks.

Your care team will show you how to give yourself injections at home. You will be given a "sharps box" to safely dispose of your used syringes or injectors.

If the medicine works well for you, you may use it on a long-term basis.

You will need to have regular blood tests while taking adalimumab to check the health of your liver and kidneys.

Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask your doctor, pharmacist or care team for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.

### If you miss a dose of adalimumab

If you forget to do your adalimumab injection, do it as soon as you remember, and take your next injection when it's due.

But if it's nearly time for your next injection, ask your care team for advice.

Do not take 2 injections at the same time to make up for a missed injection.

### If you take too much adalimumab

Taking an extra dose of adalimumab can be dangerous.

#### Urgent advice: Call NHS 111 if:

- you've taken more than your prescribed dose of adalimumab

If you're told to go to A&E, do not drive. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance.

Bring the adalimumab packet or leaflet inside it, any remaining medicine, and any other medicines you take.

## Side effects of adalimumab

Like all medicines, adalimumab can cause side effects, but not everyone gets them.

Common side effects of adalimumab include:

- itchy, red and painful skin where you inject your medicine – redness may be harder to see on brown or black skin
- headaches
- stomach pain
- feeling sick (nausea) and being sick (vomiting)
- skin rash
- muscle, joint and bone pain
- mood changes, such as feeling anxious or depressed
- blurred vision
- dizziness

If you feel dizzy or get any blurred vision after taking adalimumab, do not drive, ride a bike or use machinery until these symptoms stop.

### Non-urgent advice: Speak to a doctor or your care team if:

- you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away

They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

### Serious side effects

Adalimumab can sometimes cause serious side effects.

A common and serious side effect of adalimumab is that it makes you more likely to get infections.

These can be quite common infections, such as urinary tract infections (UTIs), colds, flu and skin infections. But you can also get rarer types of infections such as tuberculosis.

Symptoms of a possible infection include a high temperature, hot and painful skin, a cough that does not go away and pain when peeing.

Other serious side effects are rarer and include:

- an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
- Stevens-Johnson syndrome – a serious skin reaction that causes blistering of the skin, painful sores and skin peeling, as well as flu-like symptoms
- a stroke – causing symptoms such as face dropping on one side, not being able to hold both arms up and difficulty speaking
- a heart attack – causing symptoms such as chest pain that feels tight or like squeezing on your chest, feeling short of breath and sweating

#### Immediate action required: Call 999 if:

- you're taking adalimumab and think you're having anaphylaxis, stroke, heart attack or Stevens-Johnson syndrome

If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.

### More about side effects

These are not all the side effects of adalimumab.

For a full list, see the leaflet that comes with your medicine or search for your medicine on the electronic medicines compendium website.

You can report any side effects you get on the Yellow Card website

## Who can and cannot take adalimumab

Most adults can take adalimumab. It's sometimes prescribed for children.

Adalimumab is not suitable for some people. For example, it may not be suitable if:

- you've had an allergic reaction or serious side effect from adalimumab
- you have heart failure
- you have a serious infection, such as tuberculosis or hepatitis

### Breastfeeding and adalimumab

Adalimumab can be used if you're breastfeeding as only very small amounts pass into breast milk.

It's rare for breastfed babies to get any side effects from medicine you're taking, but speak to a healthcare professional if you have any concerns about your baby.

### Pregnancy and adalimumab

Tell your doctor if you're pregnant or you get pregnant while taking adalimumab.

Adalimumab can be used during pregnancy if needed. Your doctor will talk to you about the risks and benefits of taking it while you're pregnant.

If you need to take adalimumab while you're pregnant, your doctor will prescribe the lowest possible effective dose.

Find out more about taking adalimumab on the Bumps (Best Use of Medicine in Pregnancy) website

## Taking adalimumab with other medicines, food and drink

There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with adalimumab:

This includes:

- vaccines that have live viruses or bacteria in them, such as the chickenpox vaccine
- some of the medicines used in chemotherapy for cancer

Before taking adalimumab, tell your doctor or care team if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.

### Food, drink and alcohol with adalimumab

You can eat and drink as normal while taking adalimumab.
