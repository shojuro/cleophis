# Gliclazide

Gliclazide is a medicine used to lower high blood sugar levels if you have type 2 diabetes. It's only available with a prescription.

## What gliclazide is for

Gliclazide is used as a treatment for type 2 diabetes.

Type 2 diabetes happens when your body does not make enough of a hormone called insulin, or the insulin it makes does not work properly (insulin resistance). Without enough insulin, your blood sugar level can get too high (hyperglycaemia).

Gliclazide works by increasing how much insulin your body makes, which helps to lower your blood sugar level.

## How to take gliclazide

Gliclazide usually comes as tablets.

How much you need to take depends on how well your body responds to the medicine.

Most people need to take gliclazide once or twice a day. Your dose may need to be changed after you've been taking it for several weeks.

You'll usually take gliclazide long term. Do not stop taking it unless your doctor tells you to.

When taking gliclazide:

- try to take it at the same time each day – if you take it twice a day, this could be once in the morning and once in the evening
- swallow tablets whole with a glass of water
- take your tablets after a meal to help avoid stomach problems

Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you’re not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.

### If you miss a dose of gliclazide

If you forget to take a dose of gliclazide, take it as soon as you remember. If it's almost time for your next dose, skip the missed dose and then take your next dose when you normally would.

Do not take 2 doses to make up for a missed dose.

### If you take too much gliclazide

Taking an extra dose of gliclazide can be dangerous.

#### Urgent advice: Call NHS 111 if:

- you've taken more than your prescribed dose of gliclazide

If you're told to go to A&E, do not drive. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance.

Bring the gliclazide packet or leaflet inside it, any remaining medicine, and any other medicines you take.

## Side effects of gliclazide

Like all medicines, gliclazide can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.

### Common side effects

Common side effects of gliclazide include:

- stomach pain
- diarrhoea

If you take gliclazide it may cause low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia, or a "hypo"). This can make you feel dizzy, feel sick (nauseous), make you sick (vomit), feel very hungry or very tired.

If you get symptoms of a hypo, eat or drink something sugary to raise your blood sugar level quickly. If this happens often, see your GP.

If you feel sleepy or dizzy after taking gliclazide, do not drive, ride a bike or use machinery until you no longer have these symptoms.

#### Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:

- you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away

They may be able to give you advice about how to reduce any side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

### Serious side effects

Gliclazide can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.

Possible serious side effects of gliclazide include:

- an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
- a problem with your blood cells – causing symptoms such as bruising easily, a sore throat and a high temperature
- problems with your liver – causing symptoms such as feeling sick (nausea), being sick (vomiting), and yellowing of your skin and whites of your eyes (yellowing of the skin can be harder to see on brown or black skin)

#### Immediate action required: Call 999 if:

- you're taking gliclazide and you think you might be having anaphylaxis

If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.

### More about side effects

These are not all the side effects of gliclazide.

For a full list, see the leaflet that comes with your medicine or search for your medicine on the electronic medicines compendium website.

You can report any side effects you get on the Yellow Card website

## Who can and cannot take gliclazide

Most adults and children aged 12 years and over can take gliclazide, but it’s not suitable for everyone.

For example, it may not be suitable for you if:

- you've ever had an allergic reaction or serious side effect from gliclazide or a similar medicine
- you have severe kidney or liver disease
- you have acute porphyria, a condition that can affect the nervous system

Before taking gliclazide, tell your doctor if you have any medical conditions.

### Breastfeeding and gliclazide

Tell your doctor if you’re breastfeeding.

Gliclazide is not usually used while breastfeeding. Your doctor may offer a different medicine while you're breastfeeding.

### Pregnancy and gliclazide

Tell your doctor if you’re pregnant or trying to get pregnant.

Gliclazide is not usually used during pregnancy. Your doctor may offer you a different medicine while you're pregnant.

Find out more about taking antidiabetic drugs such as gliclazide during pregnancy on the Bumps (Best Use of Medicines in Pregnancy) website.

## Taking gliclazide with other medicines, food and drink

There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with gliclazide.

These include:

- other medicines for high blood sugar, such as insulin
- weight loss medicines, such as semaglutide or tirzepatide (Mounjaro)
- some antibiotics, such as clarithromycin
- some medicines for high blood pressure or heart failure

Before taking gliclazide, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.

### Food and alcohol with gliclazide

Follow your doctor’s advice about diet while taking gliclazide to reduce the chances of your blood sugar level getting too low. This may include eating a healthy diet and not skipping or delaying meals.

It’s best not to drink alcohol while taking gliclazide because it can affect your blood sugar level.
