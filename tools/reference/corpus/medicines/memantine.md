# Memantine

Memantine is a medicine used to treat Alzheimer’s disease. It's only available with a prescription.

## What memantine is for

Memantine is used to treat moderate to severe Alzheimer’s disease. It does not cure the condition but can help with the symptoms.

Alzheimer’s disease damages your brain and causes dementia, which affects thinking and memory.

If you have Alzheimer’s disease, you may have too much of a chemical called glutamate in your brain. Too much glutamate can affect your brain cells and stop them working properly.

Memantine reduces the effects of having too much glutamate in your brain. It protects the brain cells by blocking glutamate.

## How to take memantine

Memantine usually comes as tablets or liquid (oral solution) that you swallow. Some tablets are put on your tongue to dissolve (orodispersible).

Your doctor may prescribe a low dose to start with and increase it each week for a few weeks. This reduces the chances of side effects.

You should see your doctor regularly so they can check how well memantine is working. They may suggest you keep taking it for as long as it’s helping to improve your symptoms.

When taking memantine:

- take it once a day at the same time each day
- take it with or without food
- swallow tablets whole with a drink of water, or if you have orodispersible tablets let them dissolve on your tongue before swallowing – you can swallow them with or without water
- take the liquid with some water

Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.

### If you miss a dose of memantine

If you miss a dose of memantine, skip the missed dose and take your next dose at the usual time.

Do not take 2 doses to make up for a missed dose.

### If you take too much memantine

Taking an extra dose of memantine is unlikely to cause problems, but it's important to get medical advice if you take too much because it can sometimes be dangerous.

#### Urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist as soon as possible if:

- you’ve taken more than your prescribed dose of memantine

If you’re unable to speak to a pharmacist, or you took too much of your medicine on purpose, call NHS 111 for advice.

If you're told to go to A&E, do not drive. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance.

Bring the memantine packet or leaflet inside it, any remaining medicine, and any other medicines you take.

## Side effects of memantine

Like all medicines, memantine can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.

### Common side effects

Common side effects of memantine include:

- headaches
- dizziness
- balance problems
- shortness of breath
- feeling sleepy
- constipation
- high blood pressure

Your doctor will tell you if you’re able to drive or use machinery.

If you feel dizzy or sleepy after taking memantine, do not drive, ride a bike or use machinery until you no longer have these symptoms.

#### Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:

- you have shortness of breath
- you have any other side effects that bother you or do not go away

They may be able to give you advice about how to reduce any side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

### Serious effects

Memantine can sometimes cause more serious side effects, although most of them are rare.

Possible serious side effects of memantine include:

- a blood clot that prevents blood flowing through your veins and arteries, which can cause a heart attack or stroke
- inflammation in your liver – causing symptoms such as tiredness, itchy skin and yellowing of the whites of the eyes (jaundice)
- inflammation in your pancreas (acute pancreatitis) – causing symptoms such as pain in your tummy, a high temperature and feeling or being sick
- an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash and difficulty breathing
- feeling very low and having suicidal thoughts

#### Immediate action required: Call 999 if:

- you're taking memantine and think you might be having anaphylaxis, a heart attack or stroke

If you're having thoughts of harming yourself, or you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.

### More about side effects

These are not all the side effects of memantine.

For a full list, see the leaflet that came with your medicine, or search for your medicine leaflet on the electronic medicines compendium website.

You can report any side effects you get on the Yellow Card Scheme website

## Who can and cannot take memantine

Most adults can take memantine. It's not usually recommended for children and young people under the age of 18.

Memantine is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if:

- you've had an allergic reaction or serious side effect from memantine
- you have epilepsy, or you’ve had seizures (fits) in the past
- you’ve had a heart attack, or you have heart failure or uncontrolled high blood pressure (hypertension)
- you have severe kidney problems, or you’ve had severe urinary tract infections (UTIs)

Before taking memantine, tell your doctor if you have any other medical conditions.

### Breastfeeding and memantine

Tell your doctor if you're breastfeeding.

Memantine is not usually recommended when breastfeeding.

### Pregnancy and memantine

Tell your doctor if you're pregnant or you get pregnant while taking memantine.

Memantine is not usually used in pregnancy because not enough is known about its safety. But a doctor may recommend it if the benefits outweigh the risks.

## Taking memantine with other medicines, food and drink

There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with memantine.

These include:

- some medicines used to treat Parkinson’s disease, such as amantadine and apomorphine
- some medicines used to treat restless legs syndrome, such as ropinirole, pramipexole and rotigotine
- cabergoline, which is a medicine that is sometimes used to reduce high levels of a hormone called prolactin

Before taking memantine, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.

### Food, drink and alcohol with memantine

You can eat and drink as normal while taking memantine.

But tell your doctor if you want to make changes to your diet, such as changing to a vegetarian diet. Your dose of memantine may need to be changed.
